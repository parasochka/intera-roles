Cargo Overseas: the readiness number nobody could explain

Four systems, one weekly spreadsheet, and a fleet readiness figure that was always three weeks old. The problem was never the number — it was when it arrived.

Every Monday, an operations coordinator exported four lists: maintenance backlog from the planning system, open defects from inspections, certificate dates from the compliance register, and delivery confirmations from procurement email. By Wednesday there was a readiness figure. By the following Monday it was wrong again.

Nobody was doing anything careless. Each system was correct about its own part of the picture. The gap was in between: a delayed spare part sat in procurement, the overdue task sat in planning, and the connection between them existed only in someone's head.

What the first role watched

We started with one role and four checks. No migration, no new system of record — read-only access to what already existed.

  • Overdue critical maintenance per vessel, with the task and its owner.
  • Open defects older than 14 days.
  • Certificate and class dates falling inside 120 days.
  • Dependencies: parts, surveys and vendor attendance that had slipped.
Attention queue · ranked by priority and time to impact

The same four sources, ranked once, with the reason attached.

The first week found something

Two vessels carried critical tasks blocked by the same vendor. Nobody had put those two facts side by side, because they lived in different systems and different meetings. The reconciliation did it in one pass.

"The spreadsheet was never wrong. It was just always late, and it never said why."

Fleet manager, Cargo Overseas

What changed in the routine

The Monday export stopped. The weekly call now starts from the queue instead of ending with an action list, and the coordinator spends the freed morning on the vendor conversations that actually move dates.

The pattern behind the two blocked vessels is now a check of its own: when a critical spare slips more than five days on a vessel that already carries overdue maintenance, someone is told before readiness moves.

What made it work
One role, one manager, four checks — nothing company-wide. Read-only access; the planning system stayed the system of record. The check that found the problem became a permanent one.
